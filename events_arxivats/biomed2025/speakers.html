Techniques for separation and quantification of biomolecules

Aida Carreño (ICMAB-CSIC)

Aida Carreño (ICMAB-CSIC)

Title: Techniques for separation and quantification of biomolecules

Short bio: Aida Carreño, biotechnologist and doctor in Materials Science, is a nanomedicine researcher in the Nanomol-Bio group of the Institute of Materials Science of Barcelona (ICMAB-CSIC). Her research is focused on the development of nanovesicles towards the directed administration of enzymes, applied to the treatment of lysosomal diseases such as Fabry. Manuela Costa
(UAB SCAC)

Understanding Flow Cytometry: Principles and Applications

Manuela Costa (UAB SCAC)

Manuela Costa (UAB SCAC)

Title: Understanding Flow Cytometry: Principles and Applications

Short bio: Manuela Costa graduated in Biology from the University of Barcelona (UB) in 1994. Since 2002, she has been the lead technician in charge of the Flow Cytometry Service at the Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona (UAB). In 2007, this service was merged with the Cell Culture and Antibody units to create the SCAC (Cell Culture, Antibodies, and Cytometry Service), where she currently serves as the head of the Flow Cytometry Unit.

Over the years, she has collaborated on numerous research projects and has played a key role in supporting and training researchers in the use of advanced cytometry techniques.

Jordi Faraudo
(ICMAB-CSIC)

Alphafold in a nutshell

Jordi Faraudo (ICMAB-CSIC)

Jordi Faraudo (ICMAB-CSIC)

Title: Alphafold in a nutshell

Short bio: Jordi Faraudo obtuvo su doctorado con una tesis sobre Física Estadística de No Equilibrio aplicada a coloides en la Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona. A partir de una estancia postdoctoral en el Imperial College London, empezó a interesarse por el área de la Materia Condensada Blanda en la que confluyen física, química, ciencia de materiales y biología, un interés con el que ha continuado hasta la actualidad.

Actualmente es Científico Titular en el Instituto de Ciencia de Materiales de Barcelona (ICMAB-CSIC). Su línea de trabajo explora cuestiones fundamentales en el campo de la materia condensada blanda como los mecanismos de auto ensamblaje de nanopartículas, macromoléculas y polímeros, la interacción entre moléculas biológicas y materiales inorgánicos para el diseño de nuevos materiales para aplicaciones biomédicas y en el desarrollo de nuevas teorías que permitan la descripción de mecanismos de transporte de objetos nanoscópicos bajo campos externos (electrocinética, magnetoforesis) yendo más allá de la teorías clásicas.

Su visión es emplear métodos teóricos y de simulación en áreas donde a pesar de existir una gran actividad experimental e incluso industrial no hay un marco conceptual apropiado e incluso no existen todavía unas herramientas de simulación con el poder predictivo deseable. Es autor de más de un centenar de artículos científicos en estas áreas, además de numerosas comunicaciones a congresos. Es miembro del consejo editorial de dos revistas científicas y evaluador de las principales revistas científicas en física, química y nanociencia. También tiene un gran interés por la divulgación, impartiendo conferencias tanto para el público en general como para docentes de primaria y secundaria y en museos de la ciencia.

Juncal Garmendia
(IdAB-CSIC)

From mechanistics to therapeutics - experimental approaches in the pathway for antimicrobial development

Juncal Garmendia (IdAB-CSIC)

Juncal Garmendia (IdAB-CSIC)

Title: From mechanistics to therapeutics - experimental approaches in the pathway for antimicrobial development

Short bio: La Dra. Juncal Garmendia es Investigadora Cientifica del CSIC en el Instituto de Agrobiotecnología (IdAB)-CSIC, Pamplona, desde Octubre 2009. Obtuvo su Doctorado en Biología en 2001, en la Universidad Autónoma de Madrid, trabajando en los mecanismos moleculares que regulan la expresión génica en bacterias degradadoras de contaminantes aromáticos.

A continuación, comenzó su trayectoria en el estudio de mecanismos moleculares de patogénesis bacteriana. Mediante el disfrute de una long-term postdoctoral EMBO fellowship, realizó una estancia postdoctoral en el grupo del Profesor David Holden (Imperial College London-ICL, Reino Unido, años 2002-2003), utilizando Salmonella spp. como modelo de patógeno bacteriano intracelular facultativo.

Posteriormente, realizó una segunda estancia postdoctoral (2004-2006) como postdoctoral Research Associate financiado por Wellcome Trust en el laboratorio del Prof. Gad Frankel (ICL, Reino Unido), trabajando con dos patógenos bacterianos extracelulares paradigmáticos, Escherichia coli enteropatógena y enterohemorrágica.

La Dra. Garmendia continuó su trabajo en España mediante un contrato Miguel Servet del Instituto de Salud Carlos III en la Fundación Caubet-Cimera, Mallorca. Durante este período (2006-2009), su investigación se centró en los mecanismos moleculares empleados por patógenos bacterianos respiratorios.

En 2009, la Dra. Garmendia se trasladó al IdAB tras consolidar su posición como investigador permanente en el CSIC donde continua hasta la fecha, utilizando una aproximación dual (patógeno y huésped) y multidisciplinar (microbiología molecular-celular, técnicas –ómicas, biología celular e inmunología, modelos de trabajo in vitro, ex vivo e in vivo, etc.) para el desarrollo de terapias novedosas y efectivas frente patógenos prioritarios resistentes a los antibióticos.

El grupo de la Dra. Garmendia está adscrito al Centro de Investigación en Red CIBER de Enfermedades Respiratorias, y participa en las Conexiones Nanomedicina y Antimicrobial Resistance del CSIC.

Lucía Gutiérrez
(UNIZAR)

Nanoparticles in the real world: key aspects and challenges in vitro and in vivo

Lucía Gutiérrez (UNIZAR)

Lucía Gutiérrez (UNIZAR)

Title: Nanoparticles in the real world: key aspects and challenges in vitro and in vivo

Short bio: Including the completion of my doctoral thesis, I have been involved in research since 2003.

My research work has taken place in several different institutions. First at the University of Zaragoza where I did my thesis and to which I returned in 2016. And on the other hand at Queen Mary University of London (UK), the Institute of Materials Science of Madrid (ICMM-CSIC) and the University of Western Australia in Perth (Australia), where I worked as a postdoctoral researcher.

I am currently a Senior Scientist at the Institute of Nanoscience and Materials of Aragon (INMA).

During all this time, my work has been focused on the use of magnetic nanoparticles for biomedical applications. I have explored different methods for their synthesis, I have evaluated their toxicity in biological systems, paying special attention to the biodistribution processes and transformations they undergo over time, and I have developed a method based on AC magnetic susceptibility measurements for their identification and quantification in complex matrices.

Juan Pellico
(ICMAB-CSIC)

Nanomaterials for Medical Imaging

Juan Pellico (ICMAB-CSIC)

Juan Pellico (ICMAB-CSIC)

Title: Nanomaterials for Medical Imaging

Short bio: Juan Pellico Sáez obtained his BSc in chemistry from the University of Salamanca in 2010. After 2 years working as Research Assistant at the department of organic chemistry of the same university, he joined the advanced imaging unit at the Centro Nacional de Investigaciones Cardiovasculares (CNIC) in Madrid in 2012. There, he carried out his thesis under the supervision of Prof. Jesús Ruiz-Cabello and Dr. Fernando Herranz on the synthesis and radiolabelling of iron oxide nanoparticles for MRI and PET. He got the PhD degree in Chemistry from the Complutense University of Madrid (UCM) in 2016 and continued his research at CNIC with a Spanish grant. In 2018, he moved to the University of Oxford as postdoctoral research associate to work on the development of bio-responsive nanoparticles as contrast agents for MRI under the supervision of Prof. Jason J. Davis. Then, he joined to the group of Dr. Rafael T.M de Rosales at King’s College London in 2019 as senior reseracher in radiochemistry and nanotechnology. After 6 years in UK, Juan returned to Spain and recently joined the Institute of Materials Science of Barcelona with a Ramón y Cajal contract where he is leading a research line in medical imaging applications with nanomaterials. 

During these years, Juan has published more than 40 works in peer-reviewed publications, 2 patents, supervised 2 PhD theses, 7 postgraduate and 5 undergraduate theses. Moreover, his achievements have been acknowledged with awards by prestigious societies such as the European Society of Molecular Imaging, the Royal Society of Chemistry, and the UK Preclinical Imaging community. His main area of interest combines novel particulate PET tracers with the application of nanotechnology in biomedicine to develop a new generation of imaging agents for multimodal molecular imaging and particle tracking applications.

Silvia Pujals
(IQAC-CSIC)

From conventional optical microscopy to super resolution microscopy

Silvia Pujals (IQAC-CSIC)

Silvia Pujals (IQAC-CSIC)

Title: From conventional optical microscopy to super resolution microscopy

Short bio: Dr. Pujals is a tenured researcher at the Institute of Advanced Chemistry of Catalonia (IQAC-CSIC). She obtained her PhD in Organic Chemistry from Universitat de Barcelona (UB) in the field of cell-penetrating peptides. After her PhD, she moved to Kyoto University for her postdoc, where she focused on biophysics and electron microscopy. With expertise in drug delivery, peptide synthesis, and optical and electron microscopy, Silvia's scientific career has been dedicated to drug delivery systems and their study using various advanced microscopy techniques. Her background in chemistry is essential for designing and synthesizing more efficient and selective drug delivery systems, and her microscopist background is crucial for characterizing and evaluating them using both super-resolution microscopy and electron microscopy, as well as the combination of both (correlative approach).
